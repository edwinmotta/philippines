Morphological Analysis Rules & Theory

Key concepts, distinctions between Derivational and Inflectional morphemes, and valid grammatical Word Classes.

D

Derivational Morphemes

Creates or derives new words
  • Alters Meaning or Word Class: Changes either the primary meaning or shifts the grammatical word class.
  • Prefixes & Suffixes: In English, derivational morphemes can be either prefixes or suffixes.
  • Class Change Example: Quick (Adjective) + -ness = Quickness (Noun).
  • Meaning Change Example: Happy (Adjective) + un- = Unhappy (Adjective - negative connotation).
I

Inflectional Morphemes

Refines grammatical details only
  • No Category Change: Does NOT alter the word class or core meaning of a word.
  • Grammatical Function: Adds tense, number (plurality), comparison, or aspect.
  • Plural Example: Cat (Noun) + -s = Cats (Noun).
  • Tense/Degree Example: Walk + -ed = Walked (Verb); Happy + -er = Happier (Adjective).

The 4 Grammatical Word Classes in our Scope

Adjectives Describe nouns (e.g. quick, happy)
Adverbs Modify verbs/adjectives (e.g. quickly)
Nouns People, places, things (e.g. cat, action)
Verbs Actions or states (e.g. walk, run)
